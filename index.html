I find solutions to problems people don’t know they have yet — and I build training and tools that help people teach and learn well across every modality.

For more than fifteen years I’ve worked at the intersection of instructional design, faculty development, and online learning, designing instructor-led, virtual, blended, and self-paced training for adult learners and working professionals. Featured case study

Accessibility Safari: Meeting a Federal Deadline Through Training Design

Required accessibility training for faculty at Salem Community College, built ahead of the April 2026 ADA Title II web accessibility deadline — designed, delivered, evaluated, and revised using an ADDIE process.

Average course accessibility score, Fall 2025 to Spring 2026
82% → 90%
Full-time and adjunct faculty completed required training
~100
Courses now monitored, holding at 89–90%
217
New Jersey community colleges received the materials
19

  1. Analysis

    An institution-wide UDOIT audit of 164 Canvas courses surfaced more than 17,000 flagged accessibility issues — and very little remediation was happening on its own. The data pointed to a small set of recurring errors appearing in most courses, and a clear goal: 90% institutional compliance before the federal deadline.

  2. Design

    Rather than asking faculty to learn every WCAG criterion, I built the training around “spotting the Big Five” — the five errors with the greatest impact on learners:

    • Correct heading levels
    • Sufficient color contrast
    • Link text that works for screen readers
    • Accurate, reviewed video captions
    • Descriptive alternative text for images

    A safari theme turned remediation into something faculty could navigate, remember, and talk about.

  3. Development

    A slide-based training guide, an interactive practice activity, a UDOIT user guide, and a UDOIT training page in the faculty training portal, so the training lived alongside the tool faculty would actually use.

  4. Implementation

    Delivered as required training for approximately 100 full-time and adjunct faculty in January 2026, with follow-up support during remediation.

  5. Evaluation

    Average course accessibility rose from 82% across 182 courses in Fall 2025 to 90% across 206 courses in Spring 2026, meeting the institutional target in April 2026, ahead of the deadline. Scores have held at 89–90% as monitoring grew to 217 courses in Fall 2026.

  6. Revision & reach

    Adapted into an awareness-focused version with its own practice activity for all full-time staff in March 2026. In July 2026, shared through the NJCCC Distance Education Affinity Group, which connects instructional designers from all 19 New Jersey community colleges, for use at their own institutions.

Instructional design

Selected training, course & curriculum work

A cross-section of design work — professional development, course design, and institutional infrastructure.

  • Professional Development

    Adjunct & Faculty Training Portal

    A self-paced Canvas onboarding and professional development course serving 150 instructors — covering LMS best practices, accessibility compliance, instructional technology tools, and course design standards. The institution’s primary faculty-facing training resource, maintained and updated continuously.

  • Professional Development

    AI Literacy Training Course

    A five-module Canvas professional development course — Learn, Read, Practice, Teach — covering the current state of AI in higher education, curated readings, interactive tools, and assignment design frameworks. Launching institution-wide in November 2026.

  • Accessibility

    Institutional LMS Accessibility Overhaul

    Overhauled the Canvas environment for WCAG 2.1 AA compliance — theme redesign, system configuration corrections, and standardized accessible resource modules across all institutional course shells — paired with required faculty training that raised average course accessibility from 82% to 90%. Read the case study.

  • Assessment & Evaluation

    Outcomes Assessment Infrastructure

    As Co-Chair of the Outcomes Assessment Committee, oversaw Canvas Outcomes infrastructure and authored the evaluation and renewal recommendation for eLumen Insights, aligning the platform to MSCHE Standard V. The platform enabled the first assessment in years for previously unassessed programs and broadened adjunct participation; presented to the MSCHE site visit team in 2025 and to 320+ attendees of an Instructure Canvas webinar in 2026.

  • Course Design

    ENG101 OER Master Course Repository

    A collaboratively curated open educational resource repository supporting the institution’s transition away from commercial textbooks in English Composition I. Designed with faculty agency built in — not a prescribed course, but shared infrastructure instructors can draw from and contribute to.

  • Course Design

    HUM101: Introduction to the Humanities

    A concept-first, thematic humanities course built entirely on OER — the first fully OER course at Salem Community College. Multidisciplinary content across philosophy, literature, music, drama, and visual art, assessed through interpretive writing rather than content recall, and evaluated through a mixed-methods study combining a student exit survey with platform analytics.

  • Course Design

    ENG101: English Composition I

    An experimental section integrating AI literacy as a core component of composition pedagogy — developing assignment models that support authentic learning in AI-rich environments and building toward institutional adoption.

Background

About

I’ve spent most of my career at Salem Community College — the smallest community college in New Jersey, in one of its most under-resourced counties. That context shapes how I approach instructional design: equity is nonnegotiable, and good training has to work for the people with the least time and support.

My role sits at the intersection of training design, Canvas LMS administration, accessibility compliance, AI literacy curriculum development, and direct support for subject matter experts. As a long-term adjunct in English and Humanities, I evaluate instruction from both the design side and the learner-facing side — which consistently shapes what I notice and what I build.

I tend to work on things before they’re asked of me. The AI Literacy Training Course, the CLEAR tool, the Caption Wrangler — none of those started as a request. They started with a gap I noticed and the determination to close it.

I am a CidiLabs ReadyGO Ambassador, a member of the NJCCC Distance Education Affinity Group, and I have been evaluating generative AI tools in education since late 2022.
